\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

With two countries, a tariff moves a single exchange rate, so the bilateral exchange rate, the effective exchange rate, and the long-run terms of trade coincide. In a multilateral economy, tariffs change both average protection and relative treatment across foreign suppliers. Bilateral rates, the effective exchange rate, and the aggregate terms of trade can therefore respond differently, and exchange-rate adjustment depends on the full vector of trade-balance disturbances rather than on the tariffing country's bilateral relationship with the target alone.

This distinction changes the conventional tariff result. Under symmetry, a unilateral tariff appreciates the tariffing country's effective exchange rate but can depreciate its currency against untariffed bystanders when cross-origin substitution is sufficiently strong relative to domestic diversion. Retaliation strengthens the relative-treatment force and lowers the threshold for bystander depreciation. These qualitative results remain robust to moderate asymmetry. More generally, uniform protection retains the conventional local aggregate terms-of-trade and welfare result, while discriminatory protection can overturn it. In our numerical comparative statics, such reversals arise when the target is small or lightly weighted and cross-origin substitution toward larger bystanders is sufficiently strong.

The 2025 episode illustrates why the cross-country configuration of protection matters. While our model is not intended for quantitative analysis of the episode, it highlights several broader implications. A unilateral tariff can appreciate the tariffing country's currency, retaliation can reverse that response, and bilateral rates against untariffed countries can behave differently from the effective exchange rate as trade is diverted across suppliers. The effect of tariffs on ``the exchange rate'' is therefore not well defined without specifying both the exchange-rate object of interest and the configuration of protection across trading partners.
