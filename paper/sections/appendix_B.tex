\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Notation and the sign convention are those of the main text; $m\equiv\alpha_T(1-\alpha_D)>0$ at the symmetric benchmark. Unless stated otherwise, derivatives are first-order and evaluated at a balanced free-trade baseline, using the linear system of \cref{app:linear}. \Cref{sec:real} gives the real-exchange-rate extension and its proof.

\subsection{Multilateral adjustment and symmetric partners}
\label{app:proofs-framework}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:gs}]
Free trade is not required. By homogeneity and Euler's theorem, $\sum_{l\in\mathcal I}\widetilde J_{il}=\TB_i=0$ at the balanced baseline. Since $\nu_l=\chi_l-\chi_A$ with $\chi_A$ fixed, $J$ is $\widetilde J$ with row and column $A$ deleted. Its off-diagonal entries are nonnegative, and for each $i\in\mathcal I_{-A}$
\begin{equation*}
|J_{ii}|-\sum_{l\in\mathcal I_{-A},\,l\neq i}J_{il}=\widetilde J_{iA}\geq0,
\end{equation*}
strictly in at least one row. The diagonal of $-J$ is strictly positive: for $N=2$, $|J_{BB}|=\widetilde J_{BA}>0$; for $N\geq3$, $J_{ii}=0$ would force the nonnegative off-diagonal entries of row $i$ to vanish, contradicting irreducibility. Write $-J=D-K$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}(-J)>0$ and $K\geq0$. The matrix $D^{-1}K$ is nonnegative and irreducible, with every row sum at most one and at least one strictly below one. The spectral radius of an irreducible nonnegative matrix is at most its largest row sum, with equality only if all row sums are equal (Perron--Frobenius), so the spectral radius of $D^{-1}K$ is below one and
\begin{equation*}
(-J)^{-1}=(I-D^{-1}K)^{-1}D^{-1}=\sum_{k\geq0}(D^{-1}K)^kD^{-1}\geq0 .
\end{equation*}
Hence MML holds. Because $D^{-1}K$ is irreducible, the series is entrywise positive, and $\det(-J)=\det D\cdot\det(I-D^{-1}K)>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:decomposition}]
No demand system, free-trade baseline or stability condition is used. If $PMP'=M$ for every $(N-1)\times(N-1)$ permutation matrix $P$, conjugating by permutations shows that all diagonal entries are equal and all off-diagonal entries are equal, so $M=aI+b\bone\bone'$, with eigenvalue $a+(N-1)b$ on $\operatorname{span}\{\bone\}$ and $a$ on the zero-sum subspace. For $-J=a_JI+b_J\bone\bone'$ and $G^{(A)}=a_GI+b_G\bone\bone'$ this gives $j_S=a_J+(N-1)b_J$, $j_D=a_J$, $g_S=a_G+(N-1)b_G$ and $g_D=a_G$, and for $j_S,j_D\neq0$
\begin{equation*}
(-J)^{-1}=\frac{1}{j_D}I+\frac{1}{N-1}\Big(\frac{1}{j_S}-\frac{1}{j_D}\Big)\bone\bone'.
\end{equation*}
Thus $(-J)^{-1}$ and $G^{(A)}$ share the two eigenspaces, and applying them to $t=\bar t\bone+\widetilde t$ gives \cref{eq:decomp}. Partner symmetry makes the baseline flows $f_{Aj}+f_{jA}$ equal across $j$, so $w_{Aj}=1/(N-1)$; since $\widetilde t$ sums to zero, $d\nu^E_A=\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}\bar t$, and the other two parts of \cref{eq:decompparts} follow directly.

For the MML characterization, the off-diagonal entry of $J$ is $-b_J=(j_D-j_S)/(N-1)$, so the sign pattern holds if and only if $j_D\geq j_S$. If $j_D\geq j_S>0$, $-J$ is nonsingular and every entry of $(-J)^{-1}$ above is nonnegative, so MML holds. Conversely, under MML the sign pattern gives $j_D\geq j_S$, nonsingularity gives $j_S\neq0$, and $(-J)^{-1}\bone=\bone/j_S\geq0$ gives $j_S>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The symmetric nested-CES model}
\label{app:proofs-nestedces}

The baseline is the symmetric benchmark of \cref{sec:model} with $N\geq3$; for $N=2$, \cref{thm:main} reduces to \cref{eq:twocountry}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:main}]
The benchmark is invariant to relabeling $A$'s partners, so \cref{prop:decomposition} applies, and the eigenvalues \cref{eq:eigenvalues} are those of \cref{lem:jacobian} and \cref{eq:geigen}. Since $m>0$, $j_S=mM_N/(N-1)>0$ if and only if $M_N>0$, and then $j_D=Nj_S>j_S$. By \cref{prop:decomposition}, MML is therefore equivalent to $M_N>0$; if $M_N\leq0$, then $j_S\leq0$ and MML fails. If $\eta\geq1$, the bracket in \cref{eq:MN} is $1$ plus a nonnegative term plus $(N-2)\rho>0$, so $M_N>0$. Given $M_N>0$, dividing \cref{eq:geigen} by the eigenvalues of $-J$ gives $\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}=-\rho_D/M_N$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{rel}}=-\rho/(NM_N)$, and \cref{eq:lambdas} is \cref{eq:decomp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:unilateral}]
\Cref{eq:unilateral} follows from \cref{eq:lambdas} (\cref{app:linear-unilateral}). Under MML $M_N>0$, and $\rho_D>0$ since $\alpha_D\eta>0$; hence $d\nu^E_A/d\tau<0$ and, since $N\rho_D+(N-2)\rho>0$, $d\nu_B/d\tau<0$. The denominator of $d\nu_c/d\tau$ is positive, so $d\nu_c/d\tau>0$ if and only if $\rho>N\rho_D$, with $d\nu_c=0$ at $\rho=N\rho_D$. For (iv), \cref{eq:kpartners} gives each untargeted partner $k$ times the unilateral response, so the threshold is again $\rho>N\rho_D$. For a general tariff vector with $\bar t>0$, \cref{eq:reltreat} gives the stated condition; when $t_j\geq0$ its left side is nonnegative, so it can hold only if $1-N\rho_D/\rho>0$, that is, $\rho>N\rho_D$, and it then implies $t_j<\bar t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:single}]
\Cref{eq:single} is an identity (\cref{app:linear-unilateral}); under $\alpha_D\geq1/N$ both terms on its right are nonnegative, so $N\rho_D\geq\eta$, and since a bystander depreciation requires $\rho>N\rho_D$ (\cref{prop:unilateral}), it requires $\rho>\eta$. With $\rho=\eta=\sigma$, \cref{eq:unilateral} gives $d\nu_c/d\tau=(\sigma-N\rho_D)/[N(N-1)M_N]$ with $\sigma-N\rho_D=-[\sigma(N\alpha_D-1)+N(1-\alpha_D)(1-\alpha_T)]\leq0$; MML is used only to give $M_N>0$, so $d\nu_c/d\tau\leq0$. Since $\sigma>0$ and $\alpha_D<1$, the bracket, a sum of two nonnegative terms, vanishes if and only if $\alpha_D=1/N$ and $\alpha_T=1$. At $\alpha_D=1/N$, $M_N=\sigma>0$, so MML holds for every $\sigma>0$, and $d\nu_c/d\tau=-(1-\alpha_T)/(N\sigma)$, which is negative and tends to zero as $\sigma\to\infty$ if $\alpha_T<1$ and is identically zero if $\alpha_T=1$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{prop:composite} applies to any marginal tariff change that is invariant to permutations of the $N-2$ bystanders and leaves tariffs among bystanders unchanged: tariffs by $A$ or $B$ common across bystanders, tariffs by all bystanders on $A$ or on $B$ common across bystanders, and tariffs between $A$ and $B$. The mapping sets $d\tau_{AC}=d\tau_{Ac}$, $d\tau_{CA}=d\tau_{cA}$, $d\tau_{BC}=d\tau_{Bc}$, $d\tau_{CB}=d\tau_{cB}$ (the common values) and retains $d\tau_{AB},d\tau_{BA}$. In the three-region economy, $C$ has income $y_C=N-2$, home share $h_C$ with $(N-1)h_C=N-3+2\alpha_D$, weight $\tfrac12$ on each of $A$ and $B$ in its import nest, tradable share $\alpha_T$, cross-origin elasticity $\rho$ and home--import elasticity $\eta_C$; $A$ and $B$ place import weight $\frac{1}{N-1}$ on each other and $\frac{N-2}{N-1}$ on $C$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:composite}]
The argument compares the two linearized systems at the benchmark; it does not claim that the nonlinear economies coincide at finite tariffs. MML is sufficient for $M_N\neq0$ but is not otherwise used.

\emph{Common bystander response.} By \cref{lem:jacobian}, $-J=j_S(NI-\bone\bone')$ with eigenvalues $j_S$ and $Nj_S$, and $j_S=mM_N/(N-1)\neq0$, so \cref{eq:JG} has a unique solution. Baseline and shock are invariant to permutations of the bystanders, hence so is the solution: $d\nu_c=d\nu_C$ for all $c$. The equilibrium is then given by $d\TB_B=0$ and $\sum_cd\TB_c=0$ in $(d\nu_B,d\nu_C)$.

\emph{Flows and $A$'s and $B$'s demand.} In the $N$-country economy $\sum_cf_{Ac}=\sum_cf_{cA}=m\frac{N-2}{N-1}$; in the composite $f_{AC}=m\frac{N-2}{N-1}$ and, since $1-h_C=2(1-\alpha_D)/(N-1)$, $f_{CA}=\alpha_T(1-h_C)\tfrac12(N-2)=m\frac{N-2}{N-1}$; likewise for $B$, and $f_{AB}=f_{BA}=m/(N-1)$ in both, so $s_{CA}=s_{cA}=m/(N-1)$. All bystander goods face the common price change $d\nu_C+d\tau_{Ac}$ in $A$, so $A$'s import price index, its common share change $d\log s_{Ac}$ and its income response coincide with the composite's, and $\sum_cdf_{Ac}=df_{AC}$; the same holds for $B$.

\emph{Intra-bloc trade cancels.} Summing \cref{eq:tb} over bystanders, the terms $\sum_c\sum_{c'\neq c}(f_{c'c}-f_{cc'})$ cancel identically, so $\sum_c\TB_c$ has the form of $\TB_C=f_{AC}+f_{BC}-f_{CA}-f_{CB}$, and $\TB_B$ has the form of the composite's. It remains to show $\sum_cdf_{cA}=df_{CA}$ and $\sum_cdf_{cB}=df_{CB}$.

\emph{Identification of $y_C$, $h_C$ and $\eta_C$.} Since $d\log f_{cA}=d\log s_{cA}+d\log\tilde I_c-d\tau_{cA}$ is common across $c$, $\sum_cdf_{cA}=f_{CA}\,d\log f_{cA}$, which fixes $y_C=N-2$. Incomes match because $s_{CA}=s_{cA}$ and $s_{CB}=s_{cB}$, and $d\tau_{cA}=d\tau_{CA}$. For shares, measure prices in bystander currency: because bystander currencies move together and tariffs among bystanders do not change, other bystanders' goods have $dp=0$. Write $S\equiv dp_{cA}+dp_{cB}$ and $D\equiv dp_{cA}-dp_{cB}$. A bystander's import index moves by $S/(N-1)$, the composite's by $S/2$, so
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
d\log s_{cA}&=(1-\rho)\frac D2+\Big[(1-\rho)\frac{N-3}{2(N-1)}+(1-\eta)\frac{\alpha_D}{N-1}\Big]S,\\
d\log s_{CA}&=(1-\rho)\frac D2+(1-\eta_C)h_C\frac S2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The $D$ terms coincide for all $(S,D)$; the $S$ terms coincide if and only if $(1-\eta_C)(N-1)h_C=(1-\rho)(N-3)+2\alpha_D(1-\eta)$, which, with $(N-1)h_C=N-3+2\alpha_D$, is \cref{eq:etaC}. The argument for $B$'s good replaces $D$ by $-D$, and the residual share matches because shares sum to one.

\emph{Restricted Jacobian and solution.} Hence the two equilibrium conditions, including tariff terms, are identical to the composite's. Imposing $d\nu_c=d\nu_C$ on row $B$ and on the sum of the bystander rows of $-J$,
\begin{equation}
-J=j_S\begin{pmatrix}N-1&-(N-2)\\-(N-2)&2(N-2)\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det(-J)=j_S^2N(N-2),
\label{eq:reducedJ}
\end{equation}
which for $N\geq3$ is nonsingular if and only if $j_S\neq0$, whatever its sign. With disturbances $F_B$, $F_c$ and $F_C=(N-2)F_c$,
\begin{equation}
d\nu_C=\frac{F_B+(N-1)F_c}{Nj_S},\qquad d\nu_B=\frac{2F_B+(N-2)F_c}{Nj_S},
\label{eq:reducedsol}
\end{equation}
the unique solution of both systems. The composite's trade weights $\frac{1}{N-1}$ and $\frac{N-2}{N-1}$ reproduce $A$'s NEER at symmetric weights. For $N=3$, $h_C=\alpha_D$ and $\eta_C=\eta$, so $C$ is the single bystander.
\end{proof}

For the unilateral tariff, $F_B+(N-1)F_c=m(\rho-N\rho_D)/(N-1)^2$ (\cref{app:linear-unilateral}), so \cref{eq:reducedsol} reproduces the threshold of \cref{prop:unilateral}. If instead the composite keeps the mapped $y_C$, $h_C$, flows, weights and $\rho$ but replaces $\eta_C$ by $\eta$, it no longer reproduces the $N$-country economy for $N\geq4$, and its bystander threshold, computed from the linear system, falls slightly with $N$: at the US-like calibration ($\alpha_D=0.8$, $\alpha_T=0.4$, $\eta=1.5$), from $3.96$ at $N=3$ to $3.71$ at $N=8$, while $N\rho_D$ rises from $3.96$ to $10.56$. The steep $N$-dependence of $N\rho_D$ therefore comes from the bloc's internal trade, governed by $\rho$, rather than from its size.

\subsection{Retaliation, welfare, and asymmetry}
\label{app:proofs-tradewars}
\label{app:proofs-asymmetry}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:retaliation}]
Under MML $M_N>0$ (\cref{thm:main}), and the response is linear in tariffs, so responses to simultaneous tariffs superpose. (i) Shock~1, $d\tau_{AB}=d\tau$, is \cref{eq:unilateral}. Shock~2, $d\tau_{BA}=d\tau$, is the same experiment with $A$ and $B$ exchanged, so $d\log e_{BA}=-\frac{N\rho_D+(N-2)\rho}{N(N-1)M_N}d\tau$ and $d\log e_{Bc}=\frac{\rho-N\rho_D}{N(N-1)M_N}d\tau$; with $e_{AB}=1/e_{BA}$ and $e_{Ac}=e_{AB}e_{Bc}$,
\begin{equation*}
d\nu^{(2)}_B=\frac{N\rho_D+(N-2)\rho}{N(N-1)M_N}\,d\tau,\qquad d\nu^{(2)}_c=\frac{\rho}{NM_N}\,d\tau.
\end{equation*}
Adding shock~1, $d\nu_B=0$ and
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d\nu_c}{d\tau}=\frac{\rho-N\rho_D}{N(N-1)M_N}+\frac{\rho}{NM_N}=\frac{\rho-\rho_D}{(N-1)M_N}
\end{equation*}
for every bystander, so cross rates among bystanders are unchanged and $d\log e_{Bc}=d\nu_c-d\nu_B=d\nu_c$. With equal weights, $d\nu^E_A=\frac{N-2}{N-1}d\nu_c$, and $B$'s NEER, $[0+(N-2)d\nu_c]/(N-1)$, is the same. Since $M_N>0$ and $N\geq3$, every response has the sign of $\rho-\rho_D$.

(ii) $A$'s uniform tariff contributes $\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}d\tau=-\frac{\rho_D}{M_N}d\tau$ to every $d\nu_j$. Partner $j$'s tariff on $A$ is shock~2 with $j$ in the role of $B$: it contributes $\frac{N\rho_D+(N-2)\rho}{N(N-1)M_N}d\tau$ to $d\nu_j$ and $\frac{\rho}{NM_N}d\tau$ to each of the other $N-2$ partners. Summing,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d\nu_j}{d\tau}=-\frac{\rho_D}{M_N}+\frac{N\rho_D+(N-2)\rho}{N(N-1)M_N}+\frac{(N-2)\rho}{NM_N}=\frac{(N-2)(\rho-\rho_D)}{(N-1)M_N},
\end{equation*}
common to all $j$, so cross rates among partners are unchanged and $d\nu^E_A$ equals this response, with the sign of $\rho-\rho_D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:welfare}]
Indirect utility is $V_A=\kappa\,I_A/(P_{TA}^{\alpha_T}P_{NA}^{1-\alpha_T})$, so the welfare change in units of income is $d\log I_A-\alpha_T\,d\log P_{TA}-(1-\alpha_T)\,d\log P_{NA}$. By Shephard's lemma, $\alpha_T\,d\log P_{TA}=\sum_js_{Aj}\,d\log p_{Aj}$. Under the producer-price normalization $P_{NA}$ and the domestic tradable's price are fixed in $A$'s currency, and at free trade $d\log p_{Aj}=d\nu_j+d\tau_{Aj}$. Income is $I_A=w_AL_A/(1-\sum_j\theta_{Aj}s_{Aj})$ with $\theta_{Aj}=\tau_{Aj}/(1+\tau_{Aj})$; at free trade $\theta_{Aj}=0$ and $d\theta_{Aj}=d\tau_{Aj}$, so share changes drop out and $d\log I_A=\sum_js_{Aj}\,d\tau_{Aj}$. Combining,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{dU_A}{U_A'\,I_A}=\sum_js_{Aj}\,d\tau_{Aj}-\sum_js_{Aj}(d\nu_j+d\tau_{Aj})=-m_A\sum_{j\neq A}b_{Aj}\,d\nu_j,
\end{equation*}
with $m_A=\sum_js_{Aj}$ and $b_{Aj}=s_{Aj}/m_A$. The tariff terms cancel because $\tau_{Aj}=0$ at the baseline; the cancellation is first order only. Other countries' tariffs enter $A$'s prices and income only through $d\nu$, so the formula holds for any tariff vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:uniform}]
Use \cref{eq:linear} with country-specific $\eta_i$, $\rho_i$ and $\alpha_T^i$, so $s_{Aj}=\alpha_T^A(1-h_A)b_{Aj}$ and $m_A=\alpha_T^A(1-h_A)$. At $d\nu=0$, no country other than $A$ faces a price change, so every flow $f_{ij}$ with $i\neq A$ is unchanged and $F_j=f_{Aj}(d\log s_{Aj}+d\log\tilde I_A-dt)$. In $A$, $dp_{Aj}=dt$ for every $j$, so $d\log P_{MA}=dt$, the cross-origin term vanishes, $d\log s_{Aj}=(1-\eta_A)h_A\,dt$ and $d\log\tilde I_A=m_A\,dt$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
F_j=f_{Aj}\big[(1-\eta_A)h_A+\alpha_T^A(1-h_A)-1\big]dt=-f_{Aj}\,\rho_D^A\,dt,
\end{equation*}
which is \cref{eq:uniformF}; $\rho_D^A>0$ since $\eta_A>0$, $0<h_A\leq1$ and $\alpha_T^A\leq1$, so $F_j\leq0$, strictly if $f_{Aj}>0$. Under MML, \cref{eq:signrule} gives $d\nu_i\leq0$ for every $i$. If $A$ imports from every partner, $F<0$, and each row of the nonsingular nonnegative $(-J)^{-1}$ has a positive entry, so $d\nu_i<0$ for every $i$; if $J$ is irreducible, $(-J)^{-1}>0$ entrywise (\cref{lem:gs}), so $d\nu_i<0$ for every $i$ whenever $F\neq0$. By \cref{lem:welfare}, the welfare change $-m_A\sum_jb_{Aj}d\nu_j$ is nonnegative, and strictly positive in either case since the $b_{Aj}$ sum to one; the aggregate terms of trade improve for the same reason. By linearity this welfare change is $\sum_jg_j\,dt$, so $\sum_jg_j>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:threecases}]
At any $\rho$ at which MML holds, $-J$ is a nonsingular M-matrix, so $\det A(\rho)>0$ and $R_{A|B}$ has the sign of $Q_{A|B}(\rho)$. (a) At the symmetric benchmark, \cref{eq:threecountry} gives $\det A=3(mM_3/2)^2$ and $R_{A|B}=-\rho_D/(2M_3)$, so $Q=-\tfrac38m^2\rho_DM_3$, affine in $\rho$ ($q_2=0$) and negative wherever MML holds. (b) If $q_2>0$ and $q_0<0$, the product of the roots $q_0/q_2$ is negative, so there is exactly one positive root $\rho^*$, with $Q<0$ on $(0,\rho^*)$ and $Q>0$ beyond. (c) If $q_2<0$, $q_1>0$, $q_0<0$ and the discriminant is positive, the roots are real with positive sum $-q_1/q_2$ and positive product $q_0/q_2$, and the downward parabola is positive only between them. The cases are not exhaustive: other sign patterns, such as $q_0>0$, or $q_2<0$ with no positive root, are possible; in the latter case with $q_0<0$, $Q_{A|B}<0$ and the condition holds at every $\rho$ at which MML holds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Verification}
\label{app:verification}

The analytical Jacobians and tariff disturbances were compared with centered finite differences of the nonlinear model, at symmetric and asymmetric baselines. The closed forms in this appendix and in \cref{app:linear} were checked against direct solutions of the linear system, and every reported threshold was checked on both sides, with MML verified at each point.
