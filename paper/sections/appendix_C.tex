\section{Departures from the Symmetric Free-Trade Benchmark}
\label{app:departures}

This appendix extends the local results of \cref{sec:decomposition,sec:nestedces,sec:tradewars} to common and finite tariffs, real exchange rates and asymmetric primitives, and documents the U.S.--China--rest-of-world mapping and the 2025 data. \Cref{eq:response} and the MML condition apply at any balanced baseline. Values not derived analytically are numerical comparative statics computed from the exact model, with the baseline solved from primitives; they are not global theorems. MML holds at every reported root and point. Numerical values use the US-like parameters $(\alpha_D,\alpha_T,\eta)=(0.8,0.4,1.5)$, $\rho_D=1.32$.

\subsection{Common and finite tariffs}
\label{app:asym-tariff}
\label{app:asym-finite}

Suppose $A$ levies a common tariff $\tau_0$ on all $N-1$ partners, which impose no tariffs of their own. With symmetric partner primitives this baseline is invariant to every relabeling of $A$'s partners, so partner symmetry holds and \cref{prop:decomposition} applies. Tariff changes are measured as $t_j=d\log(1+\tau_{Aj})$. Let $\theta\equiv\tau_0/(1+\tau_0)$, $\mu_A$ and $\mu_P$ be the import shares of $A$'s and of a partner's tradable expenditure, $\omega$ the share of $A$'s good in a partner's import bundle, $\phi\equiv\alpha_T\mu_A/(1-\theta\alpha_T\mu_A)$ the response of $A$'s income to its import expenditure, $X\equiv f_{Aj}=f_{jA}$ the balanced bilateral flow between $A$ and each partner, and $Y\equiv f_{jk}$ the flow between two partners. Linearizing at this baseline gives
\begin{equation}
g_S=-X\rho_D(\tau_0),\qquad g_D=-X\rho,\qquad j_S=XM_S,\qquad j_D=\big(X+(N-1)Y\big)M_D,
\label{eq:tau0eigen}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
\rho_D(\tau_0)&=1-\phi(1-\theta)+(\eta-1)(1-\mu_A)(1+\phi\theta),\\
M_S&=1+(\eta-1)(1-\mu_A)(1+\phi\theta)+(\rho-1)(1-\omega)+(\eta-1)(1-\mu_P)\,\omega,\\
M_D&=1+(\rho-1)\frac{N-3+\omega}{N-2}+(\eta-1)(1-\mu_P)\frac{N-1-\omega}{N-2}.
\end{align*}
At free trade $\theta=0$, $\phi=m$, $\mu_A=\mu_P=1-\alpha_D$ and $\omega=1/(N-1)$, so $\rho_D(0)=\rho_D$ and $M_S=M_D=M_N$. Tariff disturbances and common exchange-rate movements act only on partners' trade with $A$, whereas a zero-sum movement $v$ also moves partner $j$'s rate against the other partners, on average by $(N-1)/(N-2)$ times $v_j$; hence the bases $X$ and $X+(N-1)Y$. With $x\equiv X/(X+(N-1)Y)$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}=-\rho_D(\tau_0)/M_S$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{rel}}=-x\rho/M_D$. For a further tariff on $B$, $t=dt\,e_B$, the bystander response is $d\nu_C=(\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{rel}})\,dt/(N-1)$, so $A$ depreciates against $C$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
\rho>\rho^*(\tau_0)\equiv\frac{\rho_D(\tau_0)}{x}\,\frac{M_D}{M_S}.
\label{eq:tau0thresh}
\end{equation}
At free trade $x=1/N$, so the factor $N$ in the bystander threshold is $1/x$ at the free-trade baseline. Because $\omega$, $\mu_P$, $x$ and $M_S$ depend on baseline shares that depend on $\rho$, \cref{eq:tau0thresh} is implicit in $\rho$ and is solved numerically with the tariff-ridden baseline re-solved at each $\rho$. By linearity in $t$, a marginal preferential cut for $B$, $t=-dt\,e_B$, worsens $C$'s terms of trade under the same condition.

In the reported exercises a common external tariff raises the threshold. At $N=3$, $\rho^*(\tau_0)$ is about 10 percent above its free-trade value $N\rho_D$ at $\tau_0=0.25$ and about 36 percent above it at $\tau_0=1$. The main reason is that $x$ falls: the common appreciation of $A$'s currency lowers $A$'s share of each partner's imports, so relative treatment disturbs a smaller part of the trade on which relative exchange-rate movements act. The local threshold applies to marginal cuts; in the exact model, removing the tariff on $B$ entirely requires a higher $\rho$ than a marginal cut at the same baseline.

A concentrated finite tariff works in the opposite direction. At $N=3$, starting from the symmetric free-trade benchmark and re-solving the equilibrium at each tariff, the unilateral bystander threshold falls with the size of the tariff in the computed cases: at the 2025 peak rate $\tau=1.45$ it is $2.70$, against $3\rho_D=3.96$. A larger tariff shrinks $B$'s share of $A$'s imports, so a further increase adds less average protection while continuing to divert expenditure toward $C$.

\subsection{Real exchange rates}
\label{sec:real}

Let $P_i$ be $i$'s consumer price index in its own currency and $q_{Aj}\equiv e_{Aj}P_j/P_A$ the real exchange rate, so that a rise is a real depreciation of $A$. At the symmetric benchmark the real effective exchange rate is $d\log q^E_A\equiv\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq A}d\log q_{Aj}$. With partners imposing no tariffs, to first order (\cref{app:linear-real})
\begin{equation}
d\log q_{Aj}=\Big(1-\tfrac{mN}{N-1}\Big)d\nu_j-m\bar t,\qquad
d\log q^E_A=\Big[\Big(1-\tfrac{mN}{N-1}\Big)\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}-m\Big]\bar t.
\label{eq:real}
\end{equation}
The term $-m\bar t$ is the direct effect of the tariff on $A$'s consumer price index, which offsets part of a nominal depreciation.

\begin{proposition}[Real exchange rates]
\label{prop:real}
Consider a marginal unilateral tariff $d\tau>0$ by $A$ on $B$ at the symmetric benchmark, $N\geq3$, $m>0$, partners imposing no tariffs, and define
\begin{equation*}
H_N(\rho)\equiv\Big(1-\frac{mN}{N-1}\Big)(\rho-N\rho_D)-mN\,M_N(\rho).
\end{equation*}
For every untariffed bystander $c$, if $M_N(\rho)\neq0$, $d\log q_{Ac}/d\tau=H_N(\rho)/[N(N-1)M_N(\rho)]$, so wherever MML holds the real bilateral response has the sign of $H_N(\rho)$. Since $\rho_D$ does not depend on $\rho$, $dH_N/d\rho=1-mN$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $m<1/N$, $H_N$ has the unique root
\begin{equation*}
\rho^*_q(N)=\frac{[(N-1)-mN]N\rho_D+mN[N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+1]}{(N-1)(1-mN)}.
\end{equation*}
Where MML holds, $A$ appreciates in real terms against $c$ for $\rho<\rho^*_q(N)$ and depreciates for $\rho>\rho^*_q(N)$; if MML holds at $\rho^*_q(N)$, then $\rho^*_q(N)>N\rho_D$.
\item If $m=1/N$, $H_N$ does not depend on $\rho$: $H_N=-[1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+N(N-2)\rho_D]/(N-1)$.
\item If $m>1/N$, $H_N$ is decreasing in $\rho$, so any crossing has the reverse orientation.
\item If $\eta\geq1$, so that MML holds for every $\rho>0$, and $m\geq1/N$, then $H_N(\rho)<0$ for every $\rho>0$: no positive $\rho$ produces a real depreciation against an untariffed bystander.
\item Under MML, if $m\leq(N-1)/N$, $A$'s REER appreciates. Under weak home bias this condition holds automatically, since $m=\alpha_T(1-\alpha_D)\leq(N-1)/N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

At the US-like calibration and $N=3$, $m=0.08<1/3$ and $\rho^*_q=4.93$, against the nominal threshold $3\rho_D=3.96$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:real}]
MML is used only for the sign statements in (i), (iii) and (v) and for the comparison in (i); in (iv) it follows from $\eta\geq1$. By \cref{eq:realapp} with $\bar t=d\tau/(N-1)$, $d\log q_{Ac}/d\tau=(1-\frac{mN}{N-1})\,d\nu_c/d\tau-\frac{m}{N-1}$. Substituting $d\nu_c/d\tau$ from \cref{eq:unilateral} and writing $\frac{m}{N-1}=\frac{mN\,M_N}{N(N-1)M_N}$ gives the stated response. Under MML $M_N>0$, so the response has the sign of $H_N$. Since $M_N$ in \cref{eq:MN} has slope $(N-2)/(N-1)$, $H_N$ is affine in $\rho$ with slope $1-\frac{mN}{N-1}-\frac{mN(N-2)}{N-1}=1-mN$.

(i) For $m<1/N$ the slope is positive, so $H_N$ has a unique root, is negative below it and positive above it. Multiplying $H_N=0$ by $N-1$ gives $[(N-1)-mN](\rho-N\rho_D)=mN[1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+(N-2)\rho]$; the coefficient on $\rho$ is $(N-1)-mN-mN(N-2)=(N-1)(1-mN)$, which yields $\rho^*_q(N)$. At the root $[(N-1)-mN](\rho^*_q-N\rho_D)=mN(N-1)M_N(\rho^*_q)$. For $m<1/N$ the coefficient $(N-1)-mN$ exceeds $N-2\geq1$, and if MML holds at $\rho^*_q$ the right-hand side is strictly positive because $m>0$ and $M_N(\rho^*_q)>0$; hence $\rho^*_q(N)>N\rho_D$, strictly.

(ii) For $m=1/N$ the slope is zero, and $H_N=\frac{N-2}{N-1}(\rho-N\rho_D)-\frac{1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+(N-2)\rho}{N-1}$, which reduces to the stated constant.

(iii) For $m>1/N$ the slope is negative, so $H_N$ has at most one root and is positive below it and negative above it.

(iv) If $\eta\geq1$, every term of $M_N$ in \cref{eq:MN} is nonnegative and the first is positive, so MML holds for every $\rho>0$. Since $(N-1)M_N(0)=1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)$ and $\rho_D=1+\alpha_D(\eta-1)-m$,
\begin{equation*}
(N-1)H_N(0)=-[(N-1)-mN]N\rho_D-mN[1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)]=-N\big[(N-1)(\rho_D-m)+Nm^2\big].
\end{equation*}
With $\eta\geq1$, $\rho_D-m\geq1-2m$, so
\begin{equation*}
(N-1)(\rho_D-m)+Nm^2\geq Nm^2-2(N-1)m+(N-1)=N\Big(m-\frac{N-1}{N}\Big)^2+\frac{N-1}{N}>0.
\end{equation*}
Hence $H_N(0)<0$. For $m\geq1/N$, $dH_N/d\rho\leq0$, so $H_N(\rho)\leq H_N(0)<0$ and $d\log q_{Ac}/d\tau<0$ for every $\rho>0$.

(v) Under MML $M_N>0$, and $\rho_D>0$. In \cref{eq:reer}, the first term is nonpositive if and only if $1-mN/(N-1)\geq0$, that is, $m\leq(N-1)/N$, and the second, $-m/(N-1)$, is strictly negative because $m>0$. Hence the REER strictly appreciates whenever $m\leq(N-1)/N$, including at $m=(N-1)/N$. Under weak home bias, $1-\alpha_D\leq(N-1)/N$ and $\alpha_T\leq1$, so $m=\alpha_T(1-\alpha_D)\leq(N-1)/N$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Asymmetric primitives and effective rates}
\label{app:asym-primitives}
\label{app:asym-neer}
\label{app:asym-tot}

At a general balanced baseline the coefficients of \cref{eq:linear} are the baseline shares $b_{ij}$, the home shares $h_i$ and the incomes $y_i$. The elasticity $\rho$ enters the share responses only through $(1-\rho)$, so every entry of $J$ and $F$ is affine in $\rho$, each entry of $\adj(-J)$ is a polynomial of degree at most $N-2$ in $\rho$, and each numerator $[\adj(-J)F]_i$ a polynomial of degree at most $N-1$. Since $\det(-J)>0$ under MML, a bilateral response can change sign only at a root of this numerator. At a general asymmetric baseline the $N=3$ numerator is already quadratic in $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{sign}\frac{d\log e_{AC}}{d\tau}=\operatorname{sign}\big(c_2\rho^2+c_1\rho+c_0\big),\quad c_2=\alpha_T^2\,b_{AB}b_{AC}b_{CA}b_{CB}(1-h_A)(1-h_C)y_Ay_C,
\label{eq:quadratic}
\end{equation}
where $c_1$ and $c_0$ depend on the full pattern of shares and sizes. Provided MML holds, $c_2>0$ whenever $A$ and $C$ each import from both foreign origins, so at given shares the response is positive beyond the largest positive root, which equals $3\rho_D$ at the symmetric point.

Once primitives rather than shares are held fixed, the shares themselves depend on $\rho$: at an asymmetric baseline relative prices differ across origins, and a higher $\rho$ reallocates expenditure toward the cheapest origin. A large target, whose currency depreciates to balance its trade, can then crowd $C$ out of $A$'s imports. With all other primitives symmetric and the baseline re-solved at each $\rho\in[1.02,1000]$, $e_{AC}$ reverses only for $5.99<\rho<53.8$ when $L_B=2.2$ and nowhere when $L_B=2.6$. Away from symmetry there may therefore be no bilateral threshold, one or several. Near the symmetric benchmark, the bilateral threshold rises with $A$'s origin weight on the target (a larger average-protection shock), with the target's size (which raises its share in $A$'s imports) and with home bias in $A$ alone (stronger domestic diversion), and is nearly insensitive to the bystander's size.

The trade-weighted NEER is $d\nu^E_A=\sum_{j\neq A}w_{Aj}\,d\nu_j$. At the symmetric benchmark the zero-sum component averages to zero and under MML a marginal unilateral tariff appreciates the NEER. For $N=3$ with asymmetric weights, $d\nu^E_A=w_{AB}\,d\nu_B+w_{AC}\,d\nu_C$, so the NEER can depreciate when the bystander reversal occurs and the bystander carries most of $A$'s trade. The symmetric ordering $d\nu_B<d\nu_C$ can also fail: with $L=(5.12,4.26,2.28)$, $\beta_{AB}=0.86$, $\beta_{BA}=0.65$, $\beta_{CA}=0.19$, $\alpha_D=0.89$, $\alpha_T=0.6$, $\eta=3.74$ and $\rho=1.20$, $d\nu_B-d\nu_C=0.08\,d\tau$.

The three panels of \cref{fig:tot} use the US-like parameters. In (a) and (b) the free-trade baseline is solved from primitives at $\bar\rho=3\rho_D=3.96$ and then held fixed; $\hat Q$ is $Q_{A|B}$ in \cref{eq:Q} scaled by $|q_2|$ (by $|q_1|$ when $q_2=0$), which preserves its sign and roots, and MML holds at all three baselines for $\rho\in[0.01,200]$. (a) Symmetric benchmark: $\hat Q=-(\rho+2.2)$. (b) $L=(1,0.22,1)$, $\beta_{AB}=0.2$, $\beta_{AC}=0.8$, other origin weights symmetric ($b_{AB}=0.094$): $\hat Q=\rho^2-2.6695\rho-2.2568$, with root $\rho^*=3.344$. (c) A network specified in baseline shares, with $\alpha_T=0.862$, $\eta=3.007$, home shares $(0.201,0.854,0.360)$, $(b_{AB},b_{AC})=(0.525,0.475)$, $(b_{BA},b_{BC})=(0.766,0.234)$, $(b_{CA},b_{CB})=(0.407,0.593)$ and balancing incomes $(1,5.126,0.867)$: $\hat Q=-\rho^2+31.53\rho-198.04$, so the condition fails for $8.66<\rho<22.86$.

In the family containing example (b), with $L_A=L_C=1$ and $\beta_{BA}=\beta_{CA}=0.5$, and with baselines fixed at $\bar\rho$, $q_0<0$ throughout. A positive $q_2$, and hence case (b) of \cref{prop:threecases}, arises when the target is small, has a low import weight, or both, with an approximate trade-off between the two; the root rises as the target becomes larger or more heavily weighted. For a large or heavily weighted target $q_2<0$ and $Q_{A|B}$ has no positive root, so the condition holds at every $\rho$ at which MML holds.

The three thresholds are distinct. With $\beta_{AB}=0.2$, $L_B=0.223$ (so that $w_{AC}=0.85$ at $\rho=3\rho_D$), all other primitives symmetric and the baseline re-solved at each $\rho$, $A$ depreciates against the bystander for $\rho>2.59$, its aggregate terms of trade deteriorate ($R_{A|B}>0$) for $\rho>3.40$, and its NEER depreciates for $\rho>3.97$. The bystander threshold lies below $3\rho_D$ because the target is small and lightly weighted. The NEER threshold is highest because the target's weight in $A$'s trade, $w_{AB}=0.15$, exceeds its weight in $A$'s imports, $b_{AB}=0.095$, so the favorable move against the target counts for more in the NEER. A larger bystander weight $w_{AC}$, obtained with a smaller target, lowers all three thresholds.

With $N\geq4$ and bystanders that differ from one another, partition the reduced system into the target $B$ and the set $R$ of the $N-2$ bystanders. Correspondingly, $J_{RR}$ is the block of $J$ giving the bystanders' trade-balance responses to their own rates, $J_{RB}$ the column giving their responses to $d\nu_B$, $F_R$ their tariff disturbances per unit of $d\tau$, and $d\nu_R$ the vector of $A$'s bilateral rates against the bloc. Let $q_j\equiv b_{Aj}/(1-b_{AB})$, $j\in R$, be bystander $j$'s share in $A$'s imports from the bloc, so that $q'\bone=1$ and $q'd\nu_R/d\tau=\bar R^B_{-B}$. The bystanders' rows of the local system read $J_{RB}\,d\nu_B+J_{RR}\,d\nu_R+F_R\,d\tau=0$. Under MML $-J$ is a nonsingular M-matrix, so its principal block $-J_{RR}$ is nonsingular as well. Solving for $d\nu_R$ and weighting by $q$ gives the bloc's import-weighted response,
\begin{equation}
d\bar\nu_R\equiv q'd\nu_R=-q'J_{RR}^{-1}J_{RB}\,d\nu_B-q'J_{RR}^{-1}F_R\,d\tau.
\label{eq:hetero}
\end{equation}
