\section{Departures from the Symmetric Free-Trade Benchmark}
\label{app:departures}

This appendix extends the local results of \cref{sec:decomposition,sec:nestedces,sec:tradewars} to common and finite tariffs, real exchange rates and asymmetric primitives, and documents the 2025 data. \Cref{eq:response} and the MML condition apply at any balanced baseline. Values not derived analytically are numerical comparative statics computed from the exact model, with the baseline solved from primitives; they are not global theorems. MML holds at every reported root and point. Numerical values use the US-like parameters $(\alpha_D,\alpha_T,\eta)=(0.8,0.4,1.5)$, $\rho_D=1.32$.

\subsection{Common and finite tariffs}
\label{app:asym-tariff}
\label{app:asym-finite}

In the model class of \cref{sec:model} the bystander's exchange rate is its terms of trade with $A$, $\ToT_{cA}=e_{Ac}$, so the response in \cref{eq:unilateral} is the third-country terms-of-trade effect of a discriminatory tariff, the central object of the preferential-trade-agreement literature since \citet{viner1950} and \citet{mundell1964}. In the canonical competing-exporter model, an importer $A$ that does not produce the good buys it from $B$ and $C$ under quasi-linear preferences. Let $q_j$ be exporter $j$'s price, $E_j(q_j)$ its export supply and $M_A(p_A)$ the importer's demand at domestic price $p_A$, and let $A$ levy a tariff $t_B$ on imports from $B$. With both exporters active, $p_A=q_B+t_B=q_C$, and market clearing requires $E_B(q_C-t_B)+E_C(q_C)=M_A(q_C)$. Differentiating at $t_B=0$,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{dq_C}{dt_B}=\frac{E'_B}{E'_B+E'_C-M'_A}>0,
\end{equation*}
so a tariff on $B$ always improves $C$'s terms of trade. In the nested-CES model the bystander gains only if $\rho>N\rho_D$. The difference is that $A$ also produces a competing tradable: part of the demand diverted from $B$ goes to $A$'s own good, along with the rebate's income effect, so the third-country effect survives only when cross-origin substitution is strong relative to domestic diversion.

Suppose $A$ levies a common tariff $\tau_0$ on all $N-1$ partners, which impose no tariffs of their own. With symmetric partner primitives this baseline is invariant to every relabeling of $A$'s partners, so partner symmetry holds and \cref{prop:decomposition} applies. Tariff changes are measured as $t_j=d\log(1+\tau_{Aj})$. Let $\theta\equiv\tau_0/(1+\tau_0)$, $\mu_A$ and $\mu_P$ be the import shares of $A$'s and of a partner's tradable expenditure, $\omega$ the share of $A$'s good in a partner's import bundle, $\phi\equiv\alpha_T\mu_A/(1-\theta\alpha_T\mu_A)$ the response of $A$'s income to its import expenditure, $X\equiv f_{Aj}=f_{jA}$ the balanced bilateral flow between $A$ and each partner, and $Y\equiv f_{jk}$ the flow between two partners. Linearizing at this baseline gives
\begin{equation}
g_S=-X\rho_D(\tau_0),\qquad g_D=-X\rho,\qquad j_S=XM_S,\qquad j_D=\big(X+(N-1)Y\big)M_D,
\label{eq:tau0eigen}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
\rho_D(\tau_0)&=1-\phi(1-\theta)+(\eta-1)(1-\mu_A)(1+\phi\theta),\\
M_S&=1+(\eta-1)(1-\mu_A)(1+\phi\theta)+(\rho-1)(1-\omega)+(\eta-1)(1-\mu_P)\,\omega,\\
M_D&=1+(\rho-1)\frac{N-3+\omega}{N-2}+(\eta-1)(1-\mu_P)\frac{N-1-\omega}{N-2}.
\end{align*}
At free trade $\theta=0$, $\phi=m$, $\mu_A=\mu_P=1-\alpha_D$ and $\omega=1/(N-1)$, so $\rho_D(0)=\rho_D$ and $M_S=M_D=M_N$. Tariff disturbances and common exchange-rate movements act only on partners' trade with $A$, whereas a zero-sum movement $v$ also moves partner $j$'s rate against the other partners, on average by $(N-1)/(N-2)$ times $v_j$; hence the bases $X$ and $X+(N-1)Y$. With $x\equiv X/(X+(N-1)Y)$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}=-\rho_D(\tau_0)/M_S$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{rel}}=-x\rho/M_D$. For a further tariff on $B$, $t=dt\,e_B$, the bystander response is $d\nu_C=(\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{rel}})\,dt/(N-1)$, so $A$ depreciates against $C$ if and only if
\begin{equation}
\rho>\rho^*(\tau_0)\equiv\frac{\rho_D(\tau_0)}{x}\,\frac{M_D}{M_S}.
\label{eq:tau0thresh}
\end{equation}
At free trade $x=1/N$, so the factor $N$ in the bystander threshold is $1/x$ at the free-trade baseline. Because $\omega$, $\mu_P$, $x$ and $M_S$ depend on baseline shares that depend on $\rho$, \cref{eq:tau0thresh} is implicit in $\rho$ and is solved numerically with the tariff-ridden baseline re-solved at each $\rho$. By linearity in $t$, a marginal preferential cut for $B$, $t=-dt\,e_B$, worsens $C$'s terms of trade under the same condition.

In the computed cases a positive common external tariff raises the threshold above its free-trade value $N\rho_D$, mainly because $x$ falls: the common appreciation of $A$'s currency lowers $A$'s share of each partner's imports, so relative treatment disturbs a smaller part of the trade on which relative exchange-rate movements act. A concentrated finite tariff, starting from the symmetric free-trade benchmark, works in the opposite direction and lowers the unilateral bystander threshold. The local threshold applies to marginal cuts; in the exact model, a discrete preferential cut that removes the tariff on $B$ entirely requires stronger substitution than the local threshold. The numerical values are reported in the online supplement.

\subsection{Real exchange rates}
\label{sec:real}

Let $P_i$ be $i$'s consumer price index in its own currency and $q_{Aj}\equiv e_{Aj}P_j/P_A$ the real exchange rate, so that a rise is a real depreciation of $A$. At the symmetric benchmark the real effective exchange rate is $d\log q^E_A\equiv\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq A}d\log q_{Aj}$. With partners imposing no tariffs, to first order (\cref{app:linear-real})
\begin{equation}
d\log q_{Aj}=\Big(1-\tfrac{mN}{N-1}\Big)d\nu_j-m\bar t,\qquad
d\log q^E_A=\Big[\Big(1-\tfrac{mN}{N-1}\Big)\lambda_{\mathrm{avg}}-m\Big]\bar t.
\label{eq:real}
\end{equation}
The term $-m\bar t$ is the direct effect of the tariff on $A$'s consumer price index, which offsets part of a nominal depreciation.

\begin{proposition}[Real exchange rates]
\label{prop:real}
Consider a marginal unilateral tariff $d\tau>0$ by $A$ on $B$ at the symmetric benchmark, $N\geq3$, $m>0$, partners imposing no tariffs, and define
\begin{equation*}
H_N(\rho)\equiv\Big(1-\frac{mN}{N-1}\Big)(\rho-N\rho_D)-mN\,M_N(\rho).
\end{equation*}
For every untariffed bystander $c$, if $M_N(\rho)\neq0$, $d\log q_{Ac}/d\tau=H_N(\rho)/[N(N-1)M_N(\rho)]$, so wherever MML holds the real bilateral response has the sign of $H_N(\rho)$. Since $\rho_D$ does not depend on $\rho$, $dH_N/d\rho=1-mN$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $m<1/N$, $H_N$ has the unique root
\begin{equation*}
\rho^*_q(N)=\frac{[(N-1)-mN]N\rho_D+mN[N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+1]}{(N-1)(1-mN)}.
\end{equation*}
Where MML holds, $A$ appreciates in real terms against $c$ for $\rho<\rho^*_q(N)$ and depreciates for $\rho>\rho^*_q(N)$; if MML holds at $\rho^*_q(N)$, then $\rho^*_q(N)>N\rho_D$.
\item If $m=1/N$, $H_N$ does not depend on $\rho$: $H_N=-[1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+N(N-2)\rho_D]/(N-1)$.
\item If $m>1/N$, $H_N$ is decreasing in $\rho$, so any crossing has the reverse orientation.
\item If $\eta\geq1$, so that MML holds for every $\rho>0$, and $m\geq1/N$, then $H_N(\rho)<0$ for every $\rho>0$: no positive $\rho$ produces a real depreciation against an untariffed bystander.
\item Under MML, if $m\leq(N-1)/N$, $A$'s REER appreciates. Under weak home bias this condition holds automatically, since $m=\alpha_T(1-\alpha_D)\leq(N-1)/N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

At the US-like calibration and $N=3$, $m=0.08<1/3$ and $\rho^*_q=4.93$, against the nominal threshold $3\rho_D=3.96$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:real}]
MML is used only for the sign statements in (i), (iii) and (v) and for the comparison in (i); in (iv) it follows from $\eta\geq1$. By \cref{eq:realapp} with $\bar t=d\tau/(N-1)$, $d\log q_{Ac}/d\tau=(1-\frac{mN}{N-1})\,d\nu_c/d\tau-\frac{m}{N-1}$. Substituting $d\nu_c/d\tau$ from \cref{eq:unilateral} and writing $\frac{m}{N-1}=\frac{mN\,M_N}{N(N-1)M_N}$ gives the stated response. Under MML $M_N>0$, so the response has the sign of $H_N$. Since $M_N$ in \cref{eq:MN} has slope $(N-2)/(N-1)$, $H_N$ is affine in $\rho$ with slope $1-\frac{mN}{N-1}-\frac{mN(N-2)}{N-1}=1-mN$.

(i) For $m<1/N$ the slope is positive. Multiplying $H_N=0$ by $N-1$ gives $[(N-1)-mN](\rho-N\rho_D)=mN[1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+(N-2)\rho]$; the coefficient on $\rho$ is $(N-1)-mN-mN(N-2)=(N-1)(1-mN)$, which yields $\rho^*_q(N)$. At the root $[(N-1)-mN](\rho^*_q-N\rho_D)=mN(N-1)M_N(\rho^*_q)$. For $m<1/N$ the coefficient $(N-1)-mN$ exceeds $N-2\geq1$, and if MML holds at $\rho^*_q$ the right-hand side is strictly positive because $m>0$ and $M_N(\rho^*_q)>0$; hence $\rho^*_q(N)>N\rho_D$.

(ii) For $m=1/N$ the slope is zero, and $H_N=\frac{N-2}{N-1}(\rho-N\rho_D)-\frac{1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+(N-2)\rho}{N-1}$, which reduces to the stated constant.

(iii) For $m>1/N$ the slope is negative.

(iv) If $\eta\geq1$, every term of $M_N$ in \cref{eq:MN} is nonnegative and the first is positive, so MML holds for every $\rho>0$. Since $(N-1)M_N(0)=1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)$ and $\rho_D=1+\alpha_D(\eta-1)-m$,
\begin{equation*}
(N-1)H_N(0)=-[(N-1)-mN]N\rho_D-mN[1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)]=-N\big[(N-1)(\rho_D-m)+Nm^2\big].
\end{equation*}
With $\eta\geq1$, $\rho_D-m\geq1-2m$, so
\begin{equation*}
(N-1)(\rho_D-m)+Nm^2\geq Nm^2-2(N-1)m+(N-1)=N\Big(m-\frac{N-1}{N}\Big)^2+\frac{N-1}{N}>0.
\end{equation*}
Hence $H_N(0)<0$. For $m\geq1/N$, $dH_N/d\rho\leq0$, so $H_N(\rho)\leq H_N(0)<0$ for every $\rho>0$.

(v) Under MML $M_N>0$, and $\rho_D>0$. In \cref{eq:reer}, the first term is nonpositive if and only if $m\leq(N-1)/N$, and the second, $-m/(N-1)$, is strictly negative because $m>0$. Under weak home bias, $1-\alpha_D\leq(N-1)/N$ and $\alpha_T\leq1$, so $m=\alpha_T(1-\alpha_D)\leq(N-1)/N$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Asymmetric primitives and effective rates}
\label{app:asym-primitives}
\label{app:asym-neer}
\label{app:asym-tot}

At a general balanced baseline the coefficients of \cref{eq:linear} are the baseline shares $b_{ij}$, the home shares $h_i$ and the incomes $y_i$. The elasticity $\rho$ enters the share responses only through $(1-\rho)$, so every entry of $J$ and $F$ is affine in $\rho$, each entry of $\adj(-J)$ is a polynomial of degree at most $N-2$ in $\rho$, and each numerator $[\adj(-J)F]_i$ a polynomial of degree at most $N-1$. Since $\det(-J)>0$ under MML, a bilateral response can change sign only at a root of this numerator. At a general asymmetric baseline the $N=3$ numerator is already quadratic in $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{sign}\frac{d\log e_{AC}}{d\tau}=\operatorname{sign}\big(c_2\rho^2+c_1\rho+c_0\big),\quad c_2=\alpha_T^2\,b_{AB}b_{AC}b_{CA}b_{CB}(1-h_A)(1-h_C)y_Ay_C,
\label{eq:quadratic}
\end{equation}
where $c_1$ and $c_0$ depend on the full pattern of shares and sizes. Provided MML holds, $c_2>0$ whenever $A$ and $C$ each import from both foreign origins, so at given shares the response is positive beyond the largest positive root, which equals $3\rho_D$ at the symmetric point. The aggregate numerator $Q_{A|B}$ of \cref{eq:Q} has the same structure, and its coefficients $(q_2,q_1,q_0)$ classify the aggregate terms-of-trade condition.

\begin{proposition}[Three cases]
\label{prop:threecases}
Fix a balanced free-trade baseline of the three-country model as in \cref{sec:totcondition}, and consider values $\rho>0$ at which MML holds at that baseline. The following three cases are those used in this paper; they are not an exhaustive classification of the signs of $(q_2,q_1,q_0)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Symmetric benchmark: $q_2=0$ and $Q_{A|B}(\rho)<0$ for all $\rho$. The aggregate condition always holds.
\item $q_2>0$ and $q_0<0$: $Q_{A|B}$ has exactly one positive root $\rho^*$. The condition holds for $\rho<\rho^*$ and fails for $\rho>\rho^*$.
\item $q_2<0$, $q_1>0$, $q_0<0$ and a positive discriminant: $Q_{A|B}$ has two positive roots $\rho_1<\rho_2$. The condition fails only for $\rho_1<\rho<\rho_2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:threecases}]
At any $\rho$ at which MML holds, $-J$ is a nonsingular M-matrix, so $\det A(\rho)>0$ and $R_{A|B}$ has the sign of $Q_{A|B}(\rho)$. (a) is shown in the proof of \cref{prop:reversal}. (b) If $q_2>0$ and $q_0<0$, the product of the roots $q_0/q_2$ is negative, so there is exactly one positive root $\rho^*$, with $Q<0$ on $(0,\rho^*)$ and $Q>0$ beyond. (c) If $q_2<0$, $q_1>0$, $q_0<0$ and the discriminant is positive, the roots are real with positive sum $-q_1/q_2$ and positive product $q_0/q_2$, and the downward parabola is positive only between them. The cases are not exhaustive: other sign patterns, such as $q_0>0$, or $q_2<0$ with no positive root, are possible; in the latter case with $q_0<0$, $Q_{A|B}<0$ and the condition holds at every $\rho$ at which MML holds.
\end{proof}

Case (b) is the economically central asymmetric case, shown in panel (b) of \cref{fig:tot}: the conventional aggregate effect holds at low cross-origin substitutability and is reversed above a single threshold. Case (c) requires richer network asymmetry and is reported as a robustness example in \cref{fig:tot-network}, where the reversal region is non-monotonic in $\rho$.

The panels of \cref{fig:tot} and \cref{fig:tot-network} use the US-like parameters. In (a) and (b) the free-trade baseline is solved from primitives at $\bar\rho=3\rho_D=3.96$ and then held fixed; $\hat Q$ is $Q_{A|B}$ in \cref{eq:Q} scaled by $|q_2|$ (by $|q_1|$ when $q_2=0$), which preserves its sign and roots, and MML holds at all three baselines for $\rho\in[0.01,200]$. (a) Symmetric benchmark: $\hat Q=-(\rho+2.2)$. (b) $L=(1,0.22,1)$, $\beta_{AB}=0.2$, $\beta_{AC}=0.8$, other origin weights symmetric ($b_{AB}=0.094$): $\hat Q=\rho^2-2.6695\rho-2.2568$, with root $\rho^*=3.344$. (c) A network specified in baseline shares, with $\alpha_T=0.862$, $\eta=3.007$, home shares $(0.201,0.854,0.360)$, $(b_{AB},b_{AC})=(0.525,0.475)$, $(b_{BA},b_{BC})=(0.766,0.234)$, $(b_{CA},b_{CB})=(0.407,0.593)$ and balancing incomes $(1,5.126,0.867)$: $\hat Q=-\rho^2+31.53\rho-198.04$, so the condition fails for $8.66<\rho<22.86$.

In the family containing example (b), with $L_A=L_C=1$ and $\beta_{BA}=\beta_{CA}=0.5$, and with baselines fixed at $\bar\rho$, $q_0<0$ throughout. A positive $q_2$, and hence case (b) of \cref{prop:threecases}, arises when the target is small, has a low import weight, or both, with an approximate trade-off between the two; the root rises as the target becomes larger or more heavily weighted. For a large or heavily weighted target $q_2<0$ and $Q_{A|B}$ has no positive root, so the condition holds at every $\rho$ at which MML holds.

The trade-weighted NEER is $d\nu^E_A=\sum_{j\neq A}w_{Aj}\,d\nu_j$. For $N=3$ with asymmetric weights, $d\nu^E_A=w_{AB}\,d\nu_B+w_{AC}\,d\nu_C$, so the NEER can depreciate when the bystander reversal occurs and the bystander carries most of $A$'s trade. The bilateral, aggregate terms-of-trade and NEER thresholds are distinct. With $\beta_{AB}=0.2$, $L_B=0.223$ (so that $w_{AC}=0.85$ at $\rho=3\rho_D$), all other primitives symmetric and the baseline re-solved at each $\rho$, $A$ depreciates against the bystander for $\rho>2.59$, its aggregate terms of trade deteriorate ($R_{A|B}>0$) for $\rho>3.40$, and its NEER depreciates for $\rho>3.97$. The NEER threshold is highest because the target's weight in $A$'s trade, $w_{AB}=0.15$, exceeds its weight in $A$'s imports, $b_{AB}=0.095$, so the favorable move against the target counts for more in the NEER.

With $N\geq4$ and bystanders that differ from one another, partition the reduced system into the target $B$ and the set $R$ of the $N-2$ bystanders. Correspondingly, $J_{RR}$ is the block of $J$ giving the bystanders' trade-balance responses to their own rates, $J_{RB}$ the column giving their responses to $d\nu_B$, $F_R$ their tariff disturbances per unit of $d\tau$, and $d\nu_R$ the vector of $A$'s bilateral rates against the bloc. Let $q_j\equiv b_{Aj}/(1-b_{AB})$, $j\in R$, be bystander $j$'s share in $A$'s imports from the bloc, so that $q'\bone=1$ and $q'd\nu_R/d\tau=\bar R^B_{-B}$. The bystanders' rows of the local system read $J_{RB}\,d\nu_B+J_{RR}\,d\nu_R+F_R\,d\tau=0$. Under MML $-J$ is a nonsingular M-matrix, so its principal block $-J_{RR}$ is nonsingular as well. Solving for $d\nu_R$ and weighting by $q$ gives the bloc's import-weighted response,
\begin{equation}
d\bar\nu_R\equiv q'd\nu_R=-q'J_{RR}^{-1}J_{RB}\,d\nu_B-q'J_{RR}^{-1}F_R\,d\tau.
\label{eq:hetero}
\end{equation}
The identity $R_{A|B}=b_{AB}R^B_B+(1-b_{AB})\bar R^B_{-B}$ always holds, but the bloc's average response depends on $J_{RR}$, $J_{RB}$ and $F_R$, not only on the bloc's aggregate size and weight.
