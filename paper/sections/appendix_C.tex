\section{Further Results and Data}

This appendix states sufficient conditions for the two-country sign result of \cref{sec:bilateral}, derives the real-exchange-rate results discussed in \cref{sec:composite}, and documents the 2025 data. Throughout, $e_{Aj}$ is units of $A$'s currency per unit of $j$'s currency, so a rise is a depreciation of $A$ against $j$.

\subsection{Two-country sufficient conditions}
\label{app:online-twocountry}

Let $e\equiv e_{AB}$ and let $\tau$ be $A$'s tariff on imports from $B$. If $\TB_A(e,\tau)$ is continuously differentiable and $\TB_{A,e}\neq0$, the implicit-function theorem gives
\begin{equation*}
\frac{de}{d\tau}=-\frac{\TB_{A,\tau}}{\TB_{A,e}}.
\end{equation*}
A tariff therefore appreciates $A$'s currency locally when $\TB_{A,\tau}>0$ and $\TB_{A,e}>0$. At the balanced baseline, the second condition is the Marshall--Lerner condition $\varepsilon_X+\varepsilon_M>1$. In the nested-CES model at free trade, $\TB_{A,\tau}>0$ because the domestic diversion margin $\rho_D$ is strictly positive. These are sufficient conditions rather than universal properties: alternative fiscal closures, pricing conventions, incomplete pass-through, or endogenous markups can change the underlying quantity responses and either sign.

\subsection{Real exchange rates}
\label{sec:real}

Let $P_i=P_{Ti}^{\alpha_T}P_{Ni}^{1-\alpha_T}$ be $i$'s consumer price index in its own currency and $q_{Aj}\equiv e_{Aj}P_j/P_A$ the real bilateral exchange rate, so that a rise is a real depreciation of $A$. At the symmetric benchmark the real effective exchange rate is $d\log q^E_A\equiv\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq A}d\log q_{Aj}$. With $P_{Ni}$ fixed, $d\log P_i=m\,d\log P_{Mi}$, so $d\log q_{Aj}=d\nu_j+m\,(d\log P_{Mj}-d\log P_{MA})$. Let $A$ impose $t$ and its partners impose none. With equal import weights, $d\log P_{MA}=d\nu^E_A+\bar t$ and $d\log P_{Mj}=d\nu^E_A-\frac{N}{N-1}d\nu_j$, so
\begin{equation}
d\log q_{Aj}=\Big(1-\frac{mN}{N-1}\Big)d\nu_j-m\bar t,
\label{eq:realapp}
\end{equation}
where $-m\bar t$ is the direct effect of the tariff on $A$'s consumer price index. For a unilateral tariff, $\bar t=d\tau/(N-1)$, and averaging with \cref{eq:unilateral},
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\log q^E_A}{d\tau}=-\Big(1-\frac{mN}{N-1}\Big)\frac{\rho_D}{(N-1)M_N}-\frac{m}{N-1}.
\label{eq:reer}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Real exchange rates]
\label{prop:real}
Consider a marginal unilateral tariff $d\tau>0$ by $A$ on $B$ at the symmetric benchmark, $N\geq3$, $m>0$, partners imposing no tariffs, and define
\begin{equation*}
H_N(\rho)\equiv\Big(1-\frac{mN}{N-1}\Big)(\rho-N\rho_D)-mN\,M_N(\rho).
\end{equation*}
For every untariffed bystander $c$, if $M_N(\rho)\neq0$, $d\log q_{Ac}/d\tau=H_N(\rho)/[N(N-1)M_N(\rho)]$, so wherever MML holds the real bilateral response has the sign of $H_N(\rho)$. Since $\rho_D$ does not depend on $\rho$, $dH_N/d\rho=1-mN$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $m<1/N$, $H_N$ has the unique root
\begin{equation*}
\rho^*_q(N)=\frac{[(N-1)-mN]N\rho_D+mN[N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+1]}{(N-1)(1-mN)}.
\end{equation*}
Where MML holds, $A$ appreciates in real terms against $c$ for $\rho<\rho^*_q(N)$ and depreciates for $\rho>\rho^*_q(N)$; if MML holds at $\rho^*_q(N)$, then $\rho^*_q(N)>N\rho_D$.
\item If $m=1/N$, $H_N$ does not depend on $\rho$: $H_N=-[1+N\alpha_D(\eta-1)+N(N-2)\rho_D]/(N-1)$.
\item If $m>1/N$, $H_N$ is decreasing in $\rho$, so any crossing has the reverse orientation.
\item If $\eta\geq1$, so that MML holds for every $\rho>0$, and $m\geq1/N$, then $H_N(\rho)<0$ for every $\rho>0$: no positive $\rho$ produces a real depreciation against an untariffed bystander.
\item Under MML, if $m\leq(N-1)/N$, $A$'s REER appreciates. Under weak home bias this condition holds automatically, since $m=\alpha_T(1-\alpha_D)\leq(N-1)/N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

At the US-like calibration and $N=3$, $m=0.08<1/3$ and $\rho^*_q=4.93$, against the nominal threshold $3\rho_D=3.96$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:real}]
Let $\bar t=d\tau/(N-1)$. Substituting \cref{eq:unilateral} into \cref{eq:realapp} gives the stated response $H_N/[N(N-1)M_N]$, which has the sign of $H_N$ under MML. Since $M_N$ has slope $(N-2)/(N-1)$ in $\rho$, $dH_N/d\rho=1-\frac{mN}{N-1}-\frac{mN(N-2)}{N-1}$, which equals $1-mN$.

(i) Solving $(N-1)H_N=0$, the coefficient on $\rho$ is $(N-1)(1-mN)$, which yields $\rho^*_q(N)$. At the root $[(N-1)-mN](\rho^*_q-N\rho_D)=mN(N-1)M_N(\rho^*_q)$, which is positive if MML holds there; since $(N-1)-mN>N-2>0$ for $m<1/N$, $\rho^*_q(N)>N\rho_D$. (ii) and (iii) follow from the sign of the slope, with the constant in (ii) obtained by substituting $m=1/N$ into $H_N$.

(iv) If $\eta\geq1$, every term of $M_N$ is nonnegative and the first is positive, so MML holds for every $\rho>0$, and $\rho_D-m\geq1-2m$ implies $H_N(0)<0$ (online supplement); for $m\geq1/N$, $H_N(\rho)\leq H_N(0)<0$.

(v) In \cref{eq:reer} the first term is nonpositive if and only if $m\leq(N-1)/N$, and the second is strictly negative. Under weak home bias, $m=\alpha_T(1-\alpha_D)\leq1-\alpha_D\leq(N-1)/N$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Data notes for the 2025 episode}
\label{app:episode-data}

\paragraph{Exchange-rate data.} Bilateral rates and the nominal broad dollar index (goods and services) are from the Federal Reserve H.10 release. Rates are expressed in dollars per unit of foreign currency (the CNY, CAD and MXN quotes are inverted), and the broad index is inverted, so a rise is a dollar depreciation. The index excluding China removes the renminbi from the daily log change of the broad index using China's 2025 H.10 weight (10.9 percent) and divides by one minus that weight. No daily dong series is available; the VND row of \cref{tab:phases} uses BIS monthly averages in the monthly columns and BIS end-of-month rates in the daily columns (marked $^{a}$). ``Monthly'' compares monthly averages, January with March (Phase~1), March with April (Phase~2) and July with December (Phase~3); ``daily'' runs from January~31 to April~2, April~2 to May~12 and August~6 to December~31. Panel~(a) of \cref{fig:episode} plots monthly averages relative to each series' January~2025 average; the August dong average is missing in the source.

\paragraph{Tariff data.} Panel~(b) of \cref{fig:episode} plots statutory headline tariffs above the pre-2025 baseline by effective date: US tariffs on China, China's across-the-board tariffs on the United States, US tariffs on the European Union, the 10 percent US baseline on unlisted partners, US tariffs on Canada, and Canada's tariffs on covered US goods. Rates are statutory headline rates, not trade-weighted measures. Sources are Federal Register executive orders, the Department of Finance Canada, and USDA Foreign Agricultural Service summaries of State Council Tariff Commission announcements. The complete chronology is in the online supplement.

\paragraph{Phase definitions.} Phase~1 (February~1--April~2) covers the two US tariff increases on China and China's product-specific responses. Phase~2 (April~3--May~12) covers the US--China escalation, the 10 percent baseline of April~5, from which Canada and Mexico were excluded, and the higher country-specific rates of April~9, which were suspended for every partner except China on April~10. Phase~3 (August~7--December~31) begins with the August framework rates.

\paragraph{Classification caveats.} The phase classifications are approximate. Tariffs on Canada and Mexico, partial retaliation, exclusions, and contemporaneous policy announcements mean that none of the phases is a clean experiment. Vietnam also shifted between bystander-like and targeted treatment during the year. The online supplement documents these complications, the complete tariff chronology, sector-specific factors, and permanent trade imbalances.
