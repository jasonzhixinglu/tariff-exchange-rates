The standard prediction that an import tariff appreciates the tariffing country's currency comes from a two-country setting, where the bilateral exchange rate, effective exchange rate, and the terms of trade coincide. In a multilateral setting, these measures separate and respond differently to tariffs. Under symmetric trade relationships, we show that a unilateral tariff appreciates the effective exchange rate but can depreciate the home currency against untariffed bystanders when substitution across foreign suppliers is sufficiently elastic. Tariff retaliation can instead lead both counterparts to depreciate in effective terms. Under asymmetry, uniform protection retains the conventional aggregate terms-of-trade and welfare result, while discriminatory tariffs on small or lightly weighted partners can overturn it when foreign suppliers are sufficiently close substitutes. We use the framework to contrast exchange-rate responses during the 2025 tariff episode.
