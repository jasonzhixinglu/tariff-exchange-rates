The textbook prediction that an import tariff appreciates the tariffing country's currency comes from a two-country world, in which the bilateral exchange rate, the effective exchange rate and the terms of trade are the same object. With many countries they come apart. We characterize the long-run exchange-rate response to arbitrary tariff vectors in an $N$-country trade model. To first order, the response is the product of the tariff's trade-balance disturbance at unchanged exchange rates and the inverse of the exchange-rate adjustment matrix, and we state a multilateral Marshall--Lerner condition under which that adjustment is well behaved. Under partner symmetry, any tariff vector decomposes into \emph{average protection} and \emph{relative treatment}: average protection alone moves the nominal effective exchange rate, and relative treatment alone moves bilateral rates around it.

In a nested-CES model these responses are governed by two margins: diversion toward the domestic economy, $\rho_D$, and substitution across foreign suppliers, $\rho$. A unilateral tariff always appreciates the tariffing country's effective exchange rate, yet it depreciates the currency against every untariffed bystander when $\rho>N\rho_D$, which requires that the two elasticities differ. Under retaliation the threshold falls to $\rho_D$, and both belligerents depreciate in effective terms. The symmetric $N$-country economy reduces exactly to a three-country economy with a composite bystander, which makes the role of $N$ transparent. Under partner asymmetry these clean conclusions can fail. Most notably, a tariff on a small, low-weight partner can worsen the tariffing country's aggregate terms of trade, and hence its welfare, when foreign suppliers are close substitutes. Read stylistically, the 2025 episode fits this logic: retaliation and high substitutability between China and other suppliers both point toward a weaker dollar against bystanders, while the trade channel cannot account for dollar weakness once tariffs became close to uniform.
