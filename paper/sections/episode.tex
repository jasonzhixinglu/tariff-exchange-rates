\subsection{The 2025 Tariff Episode}

The 2025 episode provides a stylized illustration of the tariff configurations considered above. The exercise is neither a calibration nor a causal test. The model is static, has flexible prices, and is evaluated around balanced trade, while observed exchange rates are high-frequency asset prices affected by many contemporaneous shocks. We therefore use the model only to compare the sign patterns associated with different tariff configurations with the observed exchange-rate movements.

The comparison is deliberately one-directional. The tariff configuration and model parameters imply a conditional sign pattern, which can then be compared with the data. Observed exchange-rate movements do not identify $\rho$, $\rho_D$, or the quantitative contribution of the trade mechanism. \Cref{tab:phases} and \cref{fig:episode} report the data; the Federal Reserve's broad dollar index is the closest counterpart of the model's NEER, and the index excluding China isolates the bystanders.

\paragraph{Phase 1: China-focused escalation (February~1--April~2).} The United States raised tariffs on China, while China responded with narrower product-specific tariffs. The United States also imposed tariffs on Canada and Mexico, and Canada retaliated, so this phase is not a clean unilateral-tariff experiment. Approximating it as a tariff concentrated on China, \cref{prop:unilateral} associates the configuration with dollar appreciation against the target and, when $\rho>N\rho_D$, depreciation against untariffed bystanders. The dollar was approximately unchanged against the renminbi and weakened against the euro and the broad index excluding China. The bystander movements have the sign associated with a unilateral tariff when $\rho>N\rho_D$, but the mixed tariff configuration and contemporaneous shocks prevent a sharper inference.

\paragraph{Phase 2: US--China retaliation (April~3--May~12).} The United States and China raised tariffs sharply against each other, while most other partners faced a common 10 percent US baseline tariff. The configuration is therefore closest to bilateral retaliation with an additional uniform component. \Cref{prop:retaliation} associates bilateral retaliation with a stable rate between the belligerents and, when $\rho>\rho_D$, depreciation against bystanders and in effective terms. During the phase, the dollar moved little against the renminbi but depreciated against the euro, the broad index excluding China, and the full broad index. This is the phase most closely aligned with the retaliation sign pattern. The comparison establishes sign consistency only; it does not identify the relevant elasticity or the contribution of the trade mechanism.

\paragraph{Phase 3: Near-uniform tariffs with residual retaliation (August~7--December~31).} US tariffs became more nearly uniform across partners, although higher rates and retaliation remained for some countries. The broader uniform component strengthens the average-protection channel, which pushes toward effective dollar appreciation, while the remaining discriminatory and retaliatory components can push in the opposite direction. The broad dollar index was approximately flat within the phase, with no clear effective appreciation. Over 2025 as a whole, the dollar depreciated substantially, with most of that movement occurring before August. The static trade mechanism therefore does not account for the full effective depreciation.

The comparison across phases is more informative than any individual episode. A configuration dominated by bilateral retaliation is associated with stronger bystander and effective depreciation forces, while broader uniform protection strengthens the average-protection force. The observed movements in Phase~2 have the sign pattern associated with retaliation, whereas Phase~3 provides no corresponding broad appreciation as protection became more uniform. These comparisons illustrate the importance of the tariff configuration without identifying tariffs as the cause of the exchange-rate movements.
